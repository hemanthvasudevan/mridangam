Masters of the Instrument

The players who set the standard

A handful of mridangam artists, across generations, defined what accompaniment could sound like — and turned a supporting role into an art form of its own.

20th century foundation

The Holy Trinity of Mridangam

Three contemporaries, each from a different lineage, are traditionally revered together as having raised mridangam accompaniment to its modern art form.

1912 – 1981

Palghat Mani Iyer

The first mridangist awarded the Sangeetha Kalanidhi (1966) and a Padma Bhushan (1971). Renowned for shadowing a soloist's exact phrasing in real time and for treating silence as a deliberate musical tool — a restraint that became the model for generations of accompanists.

Image: Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Pudukkottai school

Palani Subramania Pillai

Leading figure of the rival Pudukkottai (Manpoondia Pillai) lineage, known for intricate, densely woven rhythmic patterns. His contrasting style with Palghat Mani Iyer's is still studied as the two great schools of 20th-century mridangam playing.

Representative image: Wikimedia Commons

Third of the Trinity

Ramanathapuram C. S. Murugabhoopathy

Completes the traditional "Holy Trinity of Mridangam" alongside Mani Iyer and Palani Subramania Pillai — the three names most often cited as having defined mainstream mridangam accompaniment for the modern concert stage.

Representative image: Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Carrying the tradition forward

Later 20th & 21st century exponents

B. 1935 · Padma Vibhushan

Umayalpuram K. Sivaraman

Trained under four gurus including Palghat Mani Iyer. A prolific researcher of the instrument itself, he developed a fibreglass mridangam prototype and a mechanical jig for moulding drumheads — practical innovations alongside a celebrated performing career.

Image: Wikimedia Commons

Modern crossover

Trichy Sankaran

A globally influential exponent and teacher, credited with bringing the mridangam into academic ethnomusicology and international collaborative music while remaining deeply rooted in traditional technique.

Representative image: Wikimedia Commons

Concert & solo artistry

Karaikudi R. Mani

Known for expanding the vocabulary and presentation of the tani avartanam, including popularizing dedicated percussion ensemble concerts (laya vinyas) built entirely around rhythm.

Representative image: Wikimedia Commons
